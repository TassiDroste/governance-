Reform Window Tracker

When do governance windows open — and do the gains last?

Every significant democratic improvement episode since 1945, detected algorithmically from the V-Dem dataset: 261 windows across 138 countries. The headline findings reframe how reform happens. Improvement is episodic, not gradual — two-thirds of all democratic progress since 1945 occurred in just five per cent of country-years. Windows close fast: the median lasts two years. They open unpredictably: three-quarters open from stagnation or outright decline. And the gains mostly stick: ten years on, the median episode has kept everything it won.

Episode detection: V-Dem v16 electoral democracy index (v2x_polyarchy), 1945–2025. A window = clustered year-on-year jumps of ≥0.05 with a cumulative gain of ≥0.10 (10 points on a 0–100 scale). Retention measured ten years after the window closes. This detects democratic openings; capability-led improvements without electoral opening (e.g. Uzbekistan after 2016) follow a different path and are deliberately not captured here.  ·  Governance Compass  ·  Risk Horizon

Reading this honestly. The tracker detects windows from outcomes, so an opening becomes visible here only once it has begun to register in V-Dem’s expert coding — typically with a one-to-two-year lag. It describes base rates; it does not predict. The polyarchy index measures electoral democracy, so administrative or capability-led reform without political opening is invisible to this method by design — the Compass and Risk Horizon capture that dimension. Retention at ten years compares the level a decade after the window closed with the pre-window level; values above 100% mean the country kept its gains and added more.
